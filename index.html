SECTION 1

The problem and the idea

Some people cannot speak after stroke, ALS, or vocal-cord damage. Good communication devices can cost ₹1.5 lakh+. Letter boards are slow in emergencies.

Even without audible speech, many can still attempt words — muscles under the chin/jaw/throat may still activate. VocalBridge targets a small emergency vocabulary.

VocalBridge amplifies surface muscle signals, compares them to labeled examples, and outputs HELP (etc.) on screen/speaker when confidence is high.

Hard truths

  • Not a certified medical device.
  • Needs residual muscle activity.
  • Phase-1 is mostly REST vs HELP.
  • Unsure → silence, not a random guess.
SECTION 2

Pipeline: muscle → “Help”

  1. Electrodes pick up tiny skin voltages.
  2. AD8232 amplifies (ECG-style, repurposed).
  3. ESP32 samples the signal.
  4. Software filters + features.
  5. Decision matches labeled examples / threshold.
  6. Output screen + speaker/TTS if confident.
Skin → Electrodes → AD8232 → ESP32
→ Filter / features → Match HELP cloud
→ Confident: show + speak "Help" | Unsure: silence

It does not understand English. Training labels create a HELP region; new patterns are matched. Small differences OK; huge differences → silence.

SECTION 3

AD8232 — honest answer

AD8232 is a single-lead heart-rate/ECG front-end. We repurpose the cheap module as a biopotential amplifier for a student prototype.

“Yes it’s an ECG front-end. We’re using it on a budget to amplify surface signals for an intent prototype — not clinical EMG.”

  • Cheap, India-available, simple supply vs many dual ±9V muscle boards
  • Good enough for Phase-1 rest vs clear action
  • Limits: noise, placement, fine word separation

30-second pitch

Many people lose speech after neurological injury or disease, and good communication devices can cost over a lakh rupees. VocalBridge is our low-cost prototype that reads residual muscle activity when a person attempts a word. We amplify the skin signal, process it, and if the pattern matches HELP with enough confidence, the system displays and speaks Help. It is a student prototype with a small vocabulary and clear safety limits, aligned with better health access and reduced inequality.
